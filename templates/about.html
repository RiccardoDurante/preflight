Royalties go into the black box because metadata is broken. We fix it before the release.

The problem

Every year, hundreds of millions of dollars in music royalties are never paid to the artists who earned them. This money sits in what the industry calls the "black box" — a suspense account held by streaming platforms and collecting societies.

The reason is not fraud. It is bad metadata. When a track is delivered with a missing code, a typo in a writer's name, or a wrong percentage split, the system cannot match the stream to a verified owner. After two or three years, the unclaimed money is given to the biggest rightsholders by market share. It never reaches the real creator.

One example: the U.S. Mechanical Licensing Collective reported $424 million in unclaimed mechanical royalties in 2021 alone.

The idea

Today, the industry is reactive. Companies try to recover this money after it has already been lost, which takes years and often fails.

PreFlight is proactive. It moves the fix to the moment before the release, when correcting a code or a name costs nothing. The tool checks the metadata of a track and gives a clear report: what is wrong, why it causes revenue loss, and how to fix it.

How it could be used

This project is a proof of concept. In the real world, the same engine could be plugged into the ingestion pipeline of a music distributor, so that every track is checked at delivery time. It could also help a collecting society reduce the size of its unallocated pool, or an artist's team run a quick check before sending a release to their distributor.

About this project

PreFlight is my final project for CS50. It is a small MVP built with Python, Flask and SQLite. The goal is to show a simple but real idea from the music tech industry, and to prove that even a basic tool can point to a serious problem.
